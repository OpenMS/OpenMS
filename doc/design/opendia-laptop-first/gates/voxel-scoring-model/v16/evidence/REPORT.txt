V16 EVIDENCE-MECHANISM DIAGNOSIS
Completed 2026-11-12, raws01/02, diagnostic only

BOTTOM LINE
The data support two distinct observations, not a recovery claim:
(1) Stage D contains a real RT-disagreement minority despite median RT error zero and every row being within 10 seconds.
(2) The fixed zero-filled time grid changes apparent chromatographic coherence, and an intensity-preserving change of bin origin changes it substantially. However accepted E is MORE phase-sensitive than D, and the apparent D/null feature advantages largely disappear in all-human controls. Thus this establishes a representation sensitivity, not a D-specific failure or a usable rescue subgroup.

2. SHAPE, SPARSITY AND REPRESENTATION
The existing geometry is 32 bins over +/-15s, hence 0.9375s/bin. Raw01 existing scan metadata gives median same-isolation-window revisit 1.64635s (10th/90th percentiles 1.63178/1.67506s). A median D FWHM spans about two native revisits, although about 3.45 grid bins. Native revisit metadata was available only for raw01; no raw02 cadence was invented.

D has median six fragments with ANY positive core signal on both raws, but only two of twelve pass the existing F12 coherent-support definition, versus six for E. This distinguishes presence from a well-measured profile. Median conditional fragment dropout, among core-present fragments and time bins containing any signal, is 52%/50% for D versus 36.1%/33.3% for E. One fragment typically occurs in only a single core bin for D, versus zero for E. D has a median 25% empty core time bins, with mean 31.8% on both raws; E also has median 25% and mean about 29%. Empty bins therefore cannot simply be interpreted as missing molecules or an exclusively D defect.

Median six-fragment Pearson coherence:

The occupied-time comparison holds the actual fragment intensities fixed and excludes columns with no fragment signal. Shared zeros inflate correlation: median paired inflation is 0.1171/0.1187 for D and 0.0902/0.0885 for E. Removing empty bins is NOT an automatically better representation: empty-signal bins may be acquired scans with no matches, and D-vs-decoy AUC falls after removal. Native boundary coherence uses different boundaries/statistics, so the native-minus-binned difference alone is NOT a causal representation ablation.

A stronger intensity-preserving control pairs adjacent bins into 17 bins with identical total intensity and output dimension, changing only grouping origin (padding [0,2] versus [1,1]). Median absolute Pearson change is 0.0931/0.1035 for D, with 90th percentiles 0.2952/0.3416. Changes exceed 0.1 for 47.9%/51.7% of D. E is MORE sensitive: medians 0.1451/0.1621, and >0.1 for 60.2%/63.7%. This directly demonstrates time-aggregation dependence without new counts, amplitude scaling, reference correction or deconvolution. It does not establish a D-specific defect.
The primary plot's simpler phase comparison trims outer bins in one origin and is explicitly phase-plus-endpoint sensitivity. The mass_preserving_phase_* outputs are the stronger follow-up that removes that confounding. No outcome-based parameter optimization was done.

Shape-width proxies also differ: the total-profile half-height OCCUPIED-bin width is median 2.8125s for D versus 1.875s for E on both raws. This is quantized, can be discontiguous, and is NOT a native interpolated FWHM. Stored boundary_log_fwhm is 1.820/1.806 for D versus 1.689/1.686 for E. These stored logs are deliberately not converted to physical widths without re-verifying their producer transform. A global summed-profile maximum outside the +/-3.75s core occurs in 20.55%/20.88% of D, versus 5.73%/5.07% of E. It can reflect competing signal, not necessarily incorrect candidate RT.

Native-versus-binned library cosine is not a pure representation comparison: natE uses nearest native scans and a calibrated narrower mass tolerance, while F12 uses +/-17.5ppm and a wider core. D median natE cosine is 0.836/0.840 versus F12 0.871/0.866. E is about 0.96 in both. The discrepancy is measurable but cannot be attributed solely to binning. Existing nat/natE/natU arrays contain summary evidence, not recoverable native profile timestamps; this audit does not pretend otherwise.

AUC is midrank P(left>right)+0.5P(tie), always oriented to HIGH measured values, not flipped after looking at results. No intensity or support matching. These are conditional descriptive comparisons with residual within-bin score variation, not validation of a classifier.

Primary D/DEC comparison covers 541/866 (62.47%) and 450/929 (48.44%). Weighted mean absolute stratum-mean score gaps are 0.0904/0.0881, not zero. 4. WHAT IS DISTINCT FROM PREVIOUS FAILURES
V5 already tested mass/coelution evidence. Here D has tight-ppm support median four and |ppm| median 1.63/1.50, but those measures are not proposed again.
V9-11 already failed native fragment count/chance/uniqueness variants. Recomputing their summary contrasts is a negative-control warning, not a fourth native-count proposal.
V12 corrected profiles with a reference-trace cap and a dual-view model. This audit neither caps nor removes interference.
V13b NNLS/deconvolution had high residuals and failed its discrimination criterion. No component ownership fit is repeated.
V14/V15 amplitude-only arguments do not address a change in time aggregation with exactly fixed summed intensity. Our paired-origin check changes neither signal amplitude nor fragment identities.
The new measured issue is sensitivity to time discretization and treatment of unobserved/zero bins. It is distinct experimentally, but not yet established as the cause of D loss. The fact E is more phase-sensitive argues against a simplistic 'binning selectively destroys weak D' story.

5. SINGLE FIRST DISCRIMINATING TEST, NOT A RECOVERY PLAN
Freeze a no-training, same-raw-points representation equivalence test. Recover original native scan timestamps from existing exports plus acquisition metadata for a fixed diagnostic panel, retaining the identical six/twelve fragment lists, calibrated ppm cut, candidate apex, time support and EVERY measured intensity. Explicitly distinguish an unacquired time location from an acquired scan with no matching centroid. First reproduce the stored 32-bin array within floating-point tolerance and verify intensity conservation, then compare its coherence with a scan-aware representation of the SAME points and deterministic bin-origin changes. Do not use DIA-NN RT to center extraction, and do not select a grid or mask by D labels. Join D/E/HUMAN/ENT/DEC and confidence only after measurements.
Primary question: does scan-aware handling reduce origin dependence while preserving physical peak alignment, and does any advantage remain for all human targets versus both decoy and entrapment controls in the identical same-fold score strata? If the apparent advantage is confined to reference-selected D, or changes reflect a different mass tolerance/fragment set, stop. This is a measurement check, not another native-count feature or a learned rescue rule. A later model intervention would require its own freeze and the unchanged global pass rule, including newly gained human/entrapment counts and decoy competitor counts reported separately. No recovered IDs are claimed here.
