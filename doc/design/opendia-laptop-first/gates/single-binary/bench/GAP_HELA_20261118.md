## Why the experimental-library argument does not give parity yet
The library is the same, so the candidate set is the same. The difference is in extraction and scoring.
- OpenSWATH scores every peak group with about 40 sub-scores, including ion-mobility ones. PyProphet's SVM is trained
  semi-supervised on those scores.
- DIA-NN uses a neural network on similar, richer evidence.
- OpenDIA uses 24 features, built and checked mostly on Astral, where there is no mobility. Several of them are empty
  on diaPASEF.
- Parity with OpenSWATH needs two things:
  - adding the missing peak-group scores, with an expected ceiling from about 14,900 to 19,000 on the common set;
  - a scorer that weighs them well, about +900 on our current features.
- DIA-NN is a further step beyond that, with more IDs again at its own q.
